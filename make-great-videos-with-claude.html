Make Great Videos with Claude

Turn slides and recordings into course videos and Instagram reels with Dan Barahona.

Friday, October 23, 2026 · 1:30–4:00 p.m. · Soft finish
Different room this week: Sobrato Hall 024BC, Santa Clara University
Directions & parking for Sobrato Hall
Student host: Sandy Cai
Everyone is welcome. No coding experience or registration required.

Special start time this week: 1:30 p.m. We’ll begin with arrival, setup, and an icebreaker, followed by Dan’s tutorial around 2 p.m. This follows Sherry Turkle’s noon–1:30 p.m. conversation in Benson’s California Mission Room.

About the session

You already have the raw material: slides, a recording of yourself talking, a webinar replay. Dan Barahona, founder of AI Security University, will show how he turns those into polished course videos and Instagram reels with Claude Code and HyperFrames, starting from an empty folder and one prompt.

Along the way, we’ll cover what to share with an AI assistant, what permissions to give it, and how to check its work.

Made together in the session

We built a prompt together, Mad Libs style, during Dan’s session: a title, a genre, a character, a setting, a twist, and a final line. Here’s the resulting 20-second fictional trailer, The spicy dog-walker.

The prompt we created

Make me a 20-second movie trailer called The spicy dog-walker.

Style: a noir movie.

A dog-walker in Starbucks finds out their coffee is secretly an AI that wants their password.

Narrate it in my voice, with music that fits the mood.

End with the line: “the end of the world.”

Format it for Instagram sharing.

Try your own version by swapping the title, genre, character, setting, twist, or final line.

The original prompt asks for “my voice.” The finished example uses ElevenLabs’ synthetic James narrator, revised for a gravelly, smoky noir delivery.

Try it yourself

Follow the same workflow as Dan and make an animated video from a short starter slide deck or your own material.

  1. Open the starter kit. Download and unzip the starter kit above, then open Claude Code in that folder. The help sheet shows how on Mac and Windows.
  2. Ask Claude to set up HyperFrames. Claude installs the video tool during the session. When it finishes, type /exit, then claude again to load its new video skills.
  3. Turn your material into a video. Follow the prompts in the help sheet to turn the three starter slides and script.txt into a 30-second animated video, or use your own material.
  4. Review the result. Check the video and decide what needs to change.

Before you come

Get ready to follow along

Bring a Mac or Windows laptop where you can install software. Please try these steps before Friday; allow about 5–10 minutes for a straightforward Node.js installation, plus time to sign in to Claude Code. You do not need programming experience.

  1. Sign in to Claude Pro. AI Kitchen will reimburse SCU students, faculty, and staff for one month of Claude Pro for this session. Choose the monthly plan, keep your paid receipt, and follow the reimbursement instructions and submission form.
  2. Install Node.js, the free software HyperFrames needs. On the official Node.js download page, choose the LTS version (version 22 or newer) and the installer for your computer: .pkg for Mac or .msi for Windows. Open the download and follow the installation wizard. You may need your computer’s administrator password.

    Windows: If you see “Tools for Native Modules” or a checkbox to automatically install additional tools, leave it unchecked. Those optional developer tools are separate from Node.js and can require about 7 GB of extra space. If a later step asks for them, ask a session helper before installing them.

  3. Install Claude Code and sign in with your Claude Pro account. Follow the Claude Code quickstart and check that you can open a project. If Claude Code was already open while you installed Node.js, quit and reopen it.
  4. Check Node.js is ready. Open Terminal on Mac or Command Prompt on Windows and enter node --version. You should see a version number beginning with v22 or higher. If you see “command not found” or “not recognized,” close and reopen that window and try again.

Download the starter kit and help sheet before the session and unzip the kit on your laptop. You can also bring a few slides or a short script of your own.

HyperFrames is free. We will set it up together during the session, so you do not need to install it ahead of time.

Stuck on setup? Come at 1:30 p.m. and ask for help during arrival and setup. You are welcome to work with another participant while getting your own laptop ready.
