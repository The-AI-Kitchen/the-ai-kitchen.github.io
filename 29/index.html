Your SCU Field Guide

Patterns of Human-AI Collaboration

Start with your interests. Talk them through with AI, create a field guide to SCU opportunities, and refine it until it fits you. No coding background is needed.

0

Before the first exercise · about 1 minute

Get ready in ChatGPT Work

Setup check

Open the ChatGPT desktop app and sign in, following the instructions emailed before class. Confirm that you can start a task in Work.

  • Ready? Show that you are ready when your instructor checks in.
  • Still setting up? Raise your hand for help and reopen the emailed instructions.
  • Need more time? Pair with someone who is ready. Discuss the prompts together while one person operates the app.

Need the tool links? Open the AI Kitchen tools guide.

Use your own interests. Share only details you are comfortable including, which can be general and non-sensitive. For this exercise, use public sources and context you supply yourself. Availability is optional if timing matters to you. Keep SCU email, calendars, other connected accounts, and private files out of the task.

1

About 3 minutes, then pause for a check-in

Try a first pass with little context

Pattern · Make context visible

  1. Choose New chat → Temporary → Unpersonalized before sending a message.
  2. Replace the bracketed text below with one or two of your own interests. Keep it brief for this first pass.
  3. Keep the answer. You will compare it with your later result.
Send in your Temporary, Unpersonalized chat
I'm an SCU student interested in [one or two of your interests]. Help me find SCU clubs, events, faculty, or other opportunities that fit. Give me a brief first pass with a few suggestions. For upcoming events, look at October 8-21, 2026. You may browse public sources and include links. Use only information I share here and public sources, not connected accounts or private files.

Check-in

Which suggestions appeal to you? What would AI need to know to make them a better fit? Notice what it asks or assumes. Browsing is allowed; little personal context is the difference here.

2

Talk for a few minutes; follow the instructor’s cue

Start with a fuzzy idea

Pattern · Interview Me First

Start Voice and allow microphone access if asked. This is a live, back-and-forth conversation, separate from the microphone that only dictates text into the composer.

Use headphones if available. Speak quietly or take turns with a partner. Read or say this instruction to begin:

Say this in Voice, or send it as text
I’m going to think out loud about my interests and what I might want to explore at SCU. My ideas may be scattered or pull in different directions. Help me make sense of them. Respond with what you’re hearing, useful questions, or possible directions. I’ll react and we’ll refine the direction together. Don’t research or build the field guide yet.

Think out loud. Interests, half-formed ideas, examples, constraints, and competing directions are all useful. Listen to the response, react to what resonates, correct misunderstandings, and add context. Take turns and refine the direction together.

If Voice is unavailable: use the same approach in text, or work with a partner.

Check-in

Name one useful idea, correction, or change of direction that came from the conversation. You do not need to share personal details with the room.

3

About 2 minutes

Read and correct your brief

Pattern · Review the context

Finish the conversation by asking for a written brief. Read it and correct the interpretation before asking AI to research or build.

Ask in the same conversation
Turn our conversation into a short interest brief: interests, goal, preferences, constraints, and open questions. Separate what I told you from your suggestions. Keep it to a short paragraph. Wait while I read and correct it.

Check-in

Does this sound like you? Correct an assumption or add a missing detail. Separate what you told AI from its suggestions. Keep a copy of your reviewed brief before leaving Temporary chat.

4

Launch with the class; the first draft may take several minutes

Research and build your SCU Field Guide

Pattern · Delegate with a clear goal

  1. In the ChatGPT desktop app, start a new normal Work task.
  2. Paste your reviewed brief. Add any context that would help: what you want to get out of the experience, your preferred way to participate, relevant background, or practical constraints.
  3. Optional: if you care about attending events at particular times, add a few availability windows and your timezone yourself.
  4. Paste the combined research and HTML-building prompt below into the same request. Launch the task and save the resulting HTML page locally.

Keep the restriction to your supplied context and public sources, even if other apps are connected elsewhere in your account.

Research + build · send as one Work request with your brief
Use only my reviewed brief, context I supply in this task, and public sources. Do not connect to or read my email, calendar, other accounts, or private files. You may create and inspect the HTML for this task.

Create Your SCU Field Guide with about three recommendations that fit my interests. Choose the most useful mix of clubs, events, faculty or research, courses, and communities. For upcoming events, look at October 8-21, 2026; longer-term directions can extend beyond that. Include a less-obvious possibility if it fits. For each recommendation, explain why it fits using specific details from my brief, include a source link, and suggest a next step I could actually take. Make clear when a recommendation depends on an open question about my interests or preferences. If I supplied availability, consider it for timed events. Do not assume a schedule I have not given you.

Build one self-contained, accessible HTML page titled “Your SCU Field Guide,” with a responsive layout, useful visuals, and SCU colors. Make the recommendations easy to compare and act on. If you recommend a timed event and its details are available, include a Google Calendar link that opens a draft for me to review and save myself. Do not imply you checked my calendar or save events for me. Save the HTML locally and show me how to open it. Preview the page if a preview tool is available and tell me what you checked. Do not publish.

Check-in

Has your task started? Does it have the context that matters to you? If you are blocked, raise your hand or pair with someone who has launched.

5

Use the waiting time; return to the demo when prompted

Compare notes while it works

Pattern · Stay involved

With a partner, identify the detail in your brief most likely to make the recommendations more useful. Explain why it matters to you. What other context might change the result?

If your task is still working: discuss your brief or ask for a partial draft. If your page is ready, show your partner a recommendation and explain whether it appeals to you.

Check-in

Be ready to name one piece of context that mattered. A useful first draft is enough to start giving feedback.

6

About 8 minutes, after the class demo

Does this fit you?

Pattern · Evaluate personal fit

Open the HTML page locally. You are the expert on whether this guide is useful for you. Ask:

  1. Does it match what I care about? Which recommendation feels relevant or exciting? Which feels generic or misses the point?
  2. Did it get my context right? Did it understand your interests, preferences, and constraints? What did it assume or overlook?
  3. Would I take the next step? Is there a concrete action you would actually want to try? What would make it more appealing or practical?

Show a partner one recommendation. Say what fits and what does not. Ask them: “What could I tell AI to make this a better fit?”

When you decide to act on an event, check the date, time, location, and any sign-up requirements. A calendar link opens a draft for you to review; it does not mean AI checked your calendar.

Check-in

Choose one change to the context, recommendations, or page that would make the guide more useful to you.

7

About 6 minutes

Make one specific revision

Pattern · Give feedback with a reason

Return to the same Work task. Tell AI what to change, why it matters, and what context it should use.

Send in the same Work task
Revise [recommendation, explanation, or page feature] because [why it does not fit me or help me]. Use this context: [correction, missing detail, or preference]. Preserve what works, update the HTML, and explain how you used my feedback. Keep using only the context I supply and public sources.

Check-in

Reopen the page and inspect the changed part. Does it fit you better? Did AI use your feedback in the way you intended?

8

Brief pair share or class check-in

What changed?

Pattern · Reflect on the collaboration

  • What did the back-and-forth help clarify about what you want?
  • Which detail about you made the biggest difference to the recommendations?
  • What context did AI get wrong or still need?
  • What is one next step you might actually try?

Compare your new guide with the first pass. Focus on relevance and usefulness, as well as appearance. This is an informal reflection on your experience, rather than a controlled test of context.

Keep one next step you might actually try.
