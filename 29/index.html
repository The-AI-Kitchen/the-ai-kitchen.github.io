Your SCU Field Guide

Patterns of Human-AI Collaboration

Start with your interests. Talk them through with AI, create a field guide to SCU opportunities, and check what it makes. No coding background is needed.

0

Before the first exercise · about 1 minute

Get ready in ChatGPT Work

Setup check

Open the ChatGPT desktop app and sign in, following the instructions emailed before class. Confirm that you can start a task in Work.

  • Ready? Show that you are ready when your instructor checks in.
  • Still setting up? Raise your hand for help and reopen the emailed instructions.
  • Need more time? Pair with someone who is ready. Discuss the prompts together while one person operates the app.

Need the tool links? Open the AI Kitchen tools guide.

Use your own non-sensitive interests, or the fictional Alex example. Share only information you choose to include. For this exercise, use public sources and manually supplied availability. Keep SCU email, calendars, other connected accounts, and private files out of the task.

1

About 3 minutes, then pause for a check-in

Try a first pass with little context

Pattern · Make context visible

  1. Choose New chat → Temporary → Unpersonalized before sending a message.
  2. Replace the first sentence below with your own one-line profile, or try the psychology example.
  3. Keep the answer. You will compare it with your later result.

If you want to use Alex throughout, describe a fictional first-year Communication student interested in storytelling, technology and people, and community service.

Send in your Temporary, Unpersonalized chat
I'm a first-year psychology student interested in wellbeing, community service, and how technology affects people. Help me find SCU clubs, events, or faculty that fit my interests. Give a brief first pass with up to three suggestions for October 8-21, 2026. You may browse public sources; include source links when you do. Do not use connected accounts or private files.

Check-in

What does AI still need to know? If it asks a question, what information is it seeking? If it recommends something, what did it assume? Browsing is allowed; little personal context is the difference here.

2

Talk for a few minutes; follow the instructor’s cue

Start with a fuzzy idea

Pattern · Interview Me First

Start Voice and allow microphone access if asked. This is a live, back-and-forth conversation, separate from the microphone that only dictates text into the composer.

Use headphones if available. Speak quietly or take turns with a partner. Read or say this instruction to begin:

Say this in Voice, or send it as text
I’m going to think out loud about my interests and what I might want to explore at SCU. My ideas may be scattered or pull in different directions. Help me make sense of them. Respond with what you’re hearing, useful questions, or possible directions. I’ll react and we’ll refine the direction together. Don’t research or build the field guide yet.

Think out loud. Interests, half-formed ideas, examples, constraints, and competing directions are all useful. Listen to the response, react to what resonates, correct misunderstandings, and add context. Take turns and refine the direction together.

If Voice is unavailable: use the same approach in text, or work with a partner.

Check-in

Name one useful idea, correction, or change of direction that came from the conversation. You do not need to share personal details with the room.

3

About 2 minutes

Read and correct your brief

Pattern · Review the context

Finish the conversation by asking for a written brief. Read it and correct the interpretation before asking AI to research or build.

Ask in the same conversation
Turn our conversation into a short interest brief: interests, goal, preferences, constraints, and open questions. Separate what I told you from your suggestions. Keep it to a short paragraph. Wait while I read and correct it.

Check-in

Correct an assumption or add a missing detail. Separate what you told AI from its suggestions. Keep a copy of your reviewed brief before leaving Temporary chat.

4

Launch with the class; the first draft may take several minutes

Research and build your SCU Field Guide

Pattern · Delegate with a clear goal

  1. In the ChatGPT desktop app, start a new normal Work task.
  2. Paste your reviewed brief and a few availability windows you choose to share.
  3. Paste the combined research and HTML-building prompt below into the same request. For your own profile, replace the entire availability paragraph, including the sentence “This is invented availability for Alex, not a calendar read.” Or use Alex’s fictional brief and availability together.
  4. Launch the task and save the resulting HTML page locally.

Keep the restriction to your supplied context and public sources, even if other apps are connected elsewhere in your account.

Research + build · send as one Work request with your brief
Use only my pasted brief, supplied availability, and public sources. Do not read other local files or folders. You may inspect the HTML you create for this task. Do not connect accounts, access email or calendars, send messages, sign up, book anything, or create calendar events.

Include a less-obvious possibility if it fits. Do not invent an event, meeting time, or current opening. 5

Use the waiting time; return to the demo when prompted

Compare notes while it works

Pattern · Stay involved

With a partner, identify the one sentence in your brief most likely to make the recommendations more useful. Check whether a preference became an unsupported assumption. If your draft is already ready, open one of its sources.

If the task is still working: ask for its current draft and unknowns, or use the prepared example for the next exercise.

Open Alex’s prepared SCU Field Guide ↗Fictional profile and availability; prepared example
Check-in

Be ready to name one piece of context that mattered. A complete campus inventory is not needed to inspect a useful first draft.

6

About 8 minutes, after the class demo

Check the page and its sources

Pattern · Validate AI’s work

Open the HTML page locally. Check these three things yourself:

  1. One source fact. Open the cited source. Does it support the claim on your page?
  2. One availability claim. If an event’s time is verified, compare it with your supplied window. Missing times stay unknown. You can use the prepared example for this check.
  3. One link. Does it go where you expect? If you try a calendar link, review the event’s title, time, timezone, and location before deciding whether to save.

A calendar link opens a draft for you to review. It does not mean AI accessed your calendar or checked your actual schedule. You do not need to save an event.

Ask a partner for one sentence each: “I liked…”, “I wondered…”, and “I’d try…”.

Check-in

Mark what is verified, what is interpretation, and what is still unknown. Choose one change that would make the guide more useful.

7

About 6 minutes

Make one specific revision

Pattern · Give feedback with a reason

Return to the same Work task. Replace the bracketed parts with a specific change, your reason, and the evidence or feedback to use.

Send in the same Work task
Change [specific content or behavior] because [reason]. Use [my feedback or this public source]. Preserve what works. Inspect the changed part and summarize the change and remaining uncertainty. Keep the same supplied-context and public-source-only restrictions.

Check-in

Reopen the page and inspect the changed part yourself. Did the revision address your reason? What still needs your judgment?

8

Brief pair share or class check-in

What changed?

Pattern · Reflect on the collaboration

  • What did the back-and-forth help clarify?
  • How did the recommendations differ from the first pass?
  • What did you still have to judge?

Compare usefulness as well as appearance. This is an informal reflection on relevance, rather than a controlled test of the effect of context.

Keep one next step you might actually try.
