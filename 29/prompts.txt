YOUR SCU FIELD GUIDE
Interview Me First: from your interests to next steps
CSEN 29 classroom prompts | October 8, 2026 | ChatGPT Work desktop app
https://theaikitchen.org/29/

Use your own interests. Share only details you are comfortable including, which can be general and non-sensitive. Use public sources and context you supply yourself. Availability is optional if timing matters to you. Do not connect SCU email, calendars, other accounts, or private files.

0. GET READY

Open the ChatGPT desktop app and confirm you can start a Work task. If you need help, raise your hand or pair with someone who is ready.

1. FIRST PASS WITH LITTLE CONTEXT

Choose New chat > Temporary > Unpersonalized before sending a message. Replace the bracketed text with one or two of your own interests. Keep the answer for comparison.

I'm an SCU student interested in [one or two of your interests]. Help me find SCU clubs, events, faculty, or other opportunities that fit. Give me a brief first pass with a few suggestions. For upcoming events, look at October 8-21, 2026. You may browse public sources and include links. Use only information I share here and public sources, not connected accounts or private files.

Check in: Which suggestions appeal to you? What would AI need to know to make them a better fit? Notice what it asks or assumes. Browsing is allowed; little personal context is the difference here.

2. START WITH A FUZZY IDEA IN VOICE

Start Voice, allow microphone access if asked, and speak this instruction. Voice is a live, back-and-forth conversation, separate from the microphone that only dictates text. Use headphones if available; speak quietly or take turns with a partner.

I’m going to think out loud about my interests and what I might want to explore at SCU. My ideas may be scattered or pull in different directions. Help me make sense of them. Respond with what you’re hearing, useful questions, or possible directions. I’ll react and we’ll refine the direction together. Don’t research or build the field guide yet.

Think out loud: interests, half-formed ideas, examples, constraints, and competing directions are all useful. Listen, react to what resonates, correct misunderstandings, and add context. Take turns and refine the direction together for a few minutes. If Voice is unavailable, use the same approach in text or with a partner.

Check in: Name one useful idea, correction, or change of direction that came from the conversation. You do not need to share personal details with the room.

3. READ AND CORRECT YOUR BRIEF

Ask in the same conversation:

Turn our conversation into a short interest brief: interests, goal, preferences, constraints, and open questions. Separate what I told you from your suggestions. Keep it to a short paragraph. Wait while I read and correct it.

Check in: Does this sound like you? Correct an assumption or add a missing detail. Separate what you told AI from its suggestions. Keep a copy before leaving Temporary chat.

4. RESEARCH AND BUILD YOUR SCU FIELD GUIDE

In the ChatGPT desktop app, start a new normal Work task. Paste your reviewed brief. Add context that would help: what you want to get out of the experience, how you prefer to participate, relevant background, or practical constraints. Optional: add a few availability windows and your timezone if event timing matters. Paste this combined research and HTML-building prompt into the same request:

Use only my reviewed brief, context I supply in this task, and public sources. Do not connect to or read my email, calendar, other accounts, or private files. You may create and inspect the HTML for this task.

Create Your SCU Field Guide with about three recommendations that fit my interests. Choose the most useful mix of clubs, events, faculty or research, courses, and communities. For upcoming events, look at October 8-21, 2026; longer-term directions can extend beyond that. Include a less-obvious possibility if it fits. For each recommendation, explain why it fits using specific details from my brief, include a source link, and suggest a next step I could actually take. Make clear when a recommendation depends on an open question about my interests or preferences. If I supplied availability, consider it for timed events. Do not assume a schedule I have not given you.

Build one self-contained, accessible HTML page titled “Your SCU Field Guide,” with a responsive layout, useful visuals, and SCU colors. Make the recommendations easy to compare and act on. If you recommend a timed event and its details are available, include a Google Calendar link that opens a draft for me to review and save myself. Do not imply you checked my calendar or save events for me. Save the HTML locally and show me how to open it. Preview the page if a preview tool is available and tell me what you checked. Do not publish.

Check in: Has your task started? Does it have the context that matters to you? If blocked, raise your hand or pair with someone who has launched.

5. COMPARE NOTES WHILE IT WORKS

With a partner, identify the detail in your brief most likely to make the recommendations more useful. Explain why it matters. What other context might change the result? If your task is still working, discuss your brief or ask for a partial draft. If your page is ready, show a recommendation and explain whether it appeals to you.

6. DOES THIS FIT YOU?

Open the HTML page locally. You are the expert on whether it is useful for you:
- Does it match what I care about? Which recommendation feels relevant or exciting? Which feels generic or misses the point?
- Did it get my context right? Did it understand my interests, preferences, and constraints? What did it assume or overlook?
- Would I take the next step? What would make it more appealing or practical?

Show a partner one recommendation. Say what fits and what does not. Ask: “What could I tell AI to make this a better fit?” Choose one useful change.

When you decide to act on an event, check its date, time, location, and sign-up requirements. A calendar link opens a draft for you to review; it does not mean AI checked your calendar.

7. MAKE ONE SPECIFIC REVISION

Send in the same Work task:

Revise [recommendation, explanation, or page feature] because [why it does not fit me or help me]. Use this context: [correction, missing detail, or preference]. Preserve what works, update the HTML, and explain how you used my feedback. Keep using only the context I supply and public sources.

Check in: Reopen the page and inspect the changed part. Does it fit you better? Did AI use your feedback in the way you intended?

8. REFLECT

What did the back-and-forth help clarify about what you want? Which detail about you made the biggest difference? What context did AI get wrong or still need? What is one next step you might actually try?

Compare usefulness and relevance with the first pass, as well as appearance. This is an informal reflection on your experience, rather than a controlled test of context.
