Recipes to take home

A website involves choices about who it is for, what it should say, and what matters most. We will explore how to make those choices visible as you work with AI, and when a quick request is enough. The same approach can help with presentations, research, and other projects where a good result depends on context and judgment.

  • Understand how AI agents work. See how software connects a model to tools and a workspace so it can act, inspect results, and revise.
  • Interview me first. Ask AI to help clarify a fuzzy goal, your audience, and the choices you still need to make before it starts building.
  • Choose the right amount of planning. A quick prompt can be enough for a clear, bounded task with adequate context. When important choices are unresolved, ask questions first. For a larger job, use a plan and checkpoints to reduce costly rework.
  • Give AI context it can use. Bring source materials, examples, and your preferences together in a brief you can reuse. This is context engineering.
  • Keep human judgment in the loop. Inspect the result, decide what to keep, and steer the next revision with specific feedback.

From one useful update to an ongoing task. We will also explore scheduling a weekly check of a reviewed CV to prepare local website updates for human review.

Your 40-minute recipe

Choose a small page for yourself, a research project, a club, a lab, a program, or an event. An existing website is optional. Aim for a useful brief, a viewable first version, and one revision you can explain. We will work from 2:05 to 2:45 p.m.

  1. 2:05–2:08Name your idea. Write a one-sentence goal and identify what is still unclear.
  2. 2:08–2:16Let AI interview you. Answer questions about your audience, purpose, and priorities.
  3. 2:16–2:21Add context. Supply useful facts and an example with your reaction.
  4. 2:21–2:24Read and correct the brief. Make sure it describes what you want.
  5. 2:24–2:36Build and inspect. Ask AI to open a local preview. Click through it and check the facts.
  6. 2:36–2:45Revise with a reason. Show a partner, choose one meaningful change, and revise.

Prompts to make your own

Replace the brackets with your own idea or material. Keep the interview, brief, and revisions in the same task.

1. Start with the interview
I want to make a small website for [myself / this project / this group / this event]. Interview me one question at a time before building. Focus on who it is for, what they should understand or do, and what matters most to me. Ask up to three main questions, with a short follow-up when needed. Do not start building yet.
2. Turn the conversation into a brief
Use my answers and these materials to write a short brief: audience, purpose, essential facts, preferences, and scope. Separate facts I supplied from suggestions. Keep it to one page and a local preview. Wait while I read and correct it.
3. Build and inspect
Build the page from the brief I approved, using my source materials. Handle the preview setup and open it in the built-in browser. Check that navigation works and that the page fits a narrow screen. Check the facts against my materials and tell me what you actually checked. Save the files in this practice folder without publishing anything.
4. Make a substantive revision
I want to change [specific content or behavior] because [reason]. Use [my feedback / this updated source] to make the change. Preserve what already works. Reopen the preview, check the changed part, and summarize what you changed.

A meaningful revision could add a missing publication from a CV, update a course or event detail from a newer source, make the contact path clearer, or change which project appears first for your intended audience. Aim for one change that matters. Save the brief and website files; no public publishing is required.

One user prompt can trigger many agent steps. Watch what the AI creates, checks, and changes, then decide what still needs your judgment.
